Google surfaces notifications across multiple channels — desktop pop-ups, mobile push alerts, and email digests — to keep you informed about activity in Gmail, Drive, Calendar, and other services. Managing these settings carefully helps you stay productive without being overwhelmed by unnecessary interruptions. Each Google service has its own notification controls, and you can fine-tune them independently.

## Notification Settings Overview

The table below gives you a quick reference for where to configure notifications for each major Google service:

| Service | Notification Type | Where to Configure |
|---|---|---|
| **Gmail** | Desktop pop-ups, mobile push | Gmail → Settings gear → See all settings → General → Desktop notifications |
| **Google Drive** | Sharing, comments, edit activity | Drive → Settings gear → Notifications |
| **Google Calendar** | Event reminders, email vs. popup | Calendar → Settings gear → Settings → [Event] → Notifications |
| **Browser (Chrome)** | Site-level push notifications | Chrome → Settings → Privacy and security → Site settings → Notifications |
| **Google Meet** | Incoming call alerts | Meet → Settings → Notifications |

## Gmail Notifications

Gmail can notify you whenever new mail arrives — either on your desktop while you're using Chrome, or on your mobile device via push notifications. You can choose to be notified for all new mail or only for important messages.

### Desktop Notifications

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Gmail Settings">
    In Gmail, click the **Settings gear** icon in the top-right corner, then click **See all settings**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Go to the General tab">
    The **General** tab opens by default. Scroll down to the **Desktop notifications** section.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose your notification level">
    Select one of the following options:
    - **Mail notifications off** — no desktop alerts
    - **Important mail notifications on** — only alerts for mail Gmail marks as important
    - **New mail notifications on** — alerts for every new message
  </Step>
  <Step title="Grant browser permission if prompted">
    If this is your first time enabling desktop notifications, your browser will ask for permission. Click **Allow** to proceed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save changes">
    Scroll to the bottom of the Settings page and click **Save Changes**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Mobile Push Notifications

To manage Gmail push notifications on a mobile device, open the **Gmail app**, tap your profile picture, go to **Manage your Google Account**, then navigate to the Gmail app's in-app notification settings under **Settings → [Your account] → Notifications**.

<Note>
  On Android, you can also control Gmail notification behavior directly through your device's system settings under **Apps → Gmail → Notifications**.
</Note>

## Google Drive Notifications

Google Drive can notify you when collaborators share files with you, leave comments, suggest edits, or make changes to files you own or follow.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Drive Settings">
    In Google Drive, click the **Settings gear** icon in the top-right corner, then click **Settings**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Go to Notifications">
    In the Settings dialog, click **Notifications** in the left panel.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Toggle notification types">
    Enable or disable notifications for the following events:
    - Someone shares a file with you
    - Someone comments on your file
    - Someone replies to your comment
    - Someone requests access to your file
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  If you only want to stop notifications for a single file, open that file, click the bell icon (🔔) near the top, and select **All notifications off** for just that document.
</Tip>

## Google Calendar Notifications

Google Calendar supports two types of reminders: **popups** (on-screen alerts) and **email notifications**. You can set default reminders that apply to all new events, and also override these on a per-event basis.

### Setting Default Notifications

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Calendar Settings">
    In Google Calendar, click the **Settings gear** icon and select **Settings**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select your calendar">
    In the left sidebar under **Settings for my calendars**, click the name of the calendar you want to configure.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Scroll to Event notifications">
    Scroll down to the **Event notifications** section.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add or modify reminders">
    Click **Add notification** and choose **Email** or **Notification** (popup), then set the time before the event you'd like to be reminded (e.g., 10 minutes, 1 day).
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Per-Event Notifications

When creating or editing an individual event, scroll to the **Add notification** field at the bottom of the event editor. You can add multiple reminders per event and mix email and popup types.

| Reminder Type | How It's Delivered | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| **Popup** | On-screen alert in browser or mobile app | Quick reminders for events later the same day |
| **Email** | Sent to your Gmail inbox | Advance notice for important meetings |

## Browser Push Notifications

Chrome allows websites — including Google services — to send push notifications even when you're not actively visiting the site. You can manage these permissions at the browser level.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Chrome Settings">
    In Chrome, click the **three-dot menu** (⋮) in the top-right corner and select **Settings**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Go to Site Settings">
    Click **Privacy and security** in the left sidebar, then click **Site settings**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open Notifications">
    Under **Permissions**, click **Notifications**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Manage notification permissions">
    You'll see a list of sites allowed or blocked from sending notifications. You can:
    - Toggle the default behavior for all sites
    - Add a specific site to the **Allowed** or **Don't allow** list
    - Remove or reset permissions for any existing entry
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  Some Google services require browser notification permissions to deliver real-time alerts. If you block all notifications at the browser level, in-browser alerts for Gmail, Calendar, and Meet will stop working even if they are enabled within each app's settings.
</Warning>
