Google Workspace is built around the idea that your best work happens together. Whether your team is in the same office or spread across time zones, Workspace apps let multiple people write in the same document simultaneously, leave feedback with comments, track every change through version history, and jump into a video call without leaving the browser tab. This guide walks you through the core collaboration features so you can get your team working in sync.

## Share a File in Google Drive

Sharing a file gives teammates access to view, comment on, or edit it directly in their browser — no attachments or email threads required.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the file">
    Open the file in Google Drive, Docs, Sheets, or Slides. You can share from the Drive file list or from inside the document itself.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click Share">
    Click the **Share** button in the top-right corner of the screen. The sharing dialog will open.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter email addresses">
    Type the email addresses of the people you want to invite. You can add multiple addresses separated by commas. Google will autocomplete addresses from your organization's directory.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set a role">
    Use the role dropdown next to each person's email to choose their permission level:
    - **Viewer** — can read the file but cannot make changes or comments.
    - **Commenter** — can read and leave comments but cannot edit content.
    - **Editor** — can read, comment, and make direct edits to the file.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send the invitation">
    Optionally add a message, then click **Send**. Each invitee will receive an email with a link to the file.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Real-Time Co-Editing

Once a file is shared with editor access, multiple people can open and edit it at the same time. Each collaborator's cursor appears in a distinct color with their name attached, so you always know who is editing which section. Changes are saved to Google's servers automatically as you type — there is no Save button to click and no risk of one person's version overwriting another's.

To see the full history of changes, open **File → Version history → See version history**. Google Docs, Sheets, and Slides record every edit with a timestamp and the name of the person who made it. You can click any earlier version to preview it or restore it as the current version.

## Comments and Suggestions

**Leaving a comment:** Highlight the text you want to discuss, then click the comment icon that appears in the margin, or press `Ctrl+Alt+M` (Windows) / `Cmd+Option+M` (Mac). Type your note and click **Comment**. To draw a specific person's attention, type `@` followed by their name or email address — they will receive an email notification. Anyone with commenter or editor access can reply to a comment thread; click **Resolve** when the issue is addressed.

**Suggesting edits:** In Google Docs, switch from **Editing** mode to **Suggesting** mode using the dropdown in the top-right corner of the document (the pencil icon). Every change you make will appear as a colored suggestion rather than a direct edit. The document owner and editors can then accept or reject each suggestion individually with one click, keeping a clean record of proposed versus accepted changes.

## Google Meet

You can start or join a video meeting without leaving your document or calendar.

- **From a Google Doc:** Click **Tools → Collaborate with others → Open Meet in this document**. A Meet panel opens on the right side of the screen, so you can talk while editing together.
- **From Google Calendar:** Open a calendar event and click the **Join with Google Meet** button. Calendar automatically generates a Meet link for every event you create — share the link with attendees ahead of time so they can join with one click.

During a Meet call you can present your screen, use breakout rooms, enable live captions, and record the session to Google Drive (available on Business and Enterprise plans).

## Managing Access

To review or change who has access to a file, open the **Share** dialog and click **Share** again. You will see a list of everyone with access and their current role.

- **Change a role:** Click the role dropdown next to a person's name and select a new permission level.
- **Remove access:** Click the role dropdown and select **Remove access**. The person will no longer be able to open the file via their shared link.
- **Transfer ownership:** Click the role dropdown next to an editor and select **Transfer ownership**. Ownership can only be transferred to someone inside the same Google Workspace organization. The original owner becomes an editor after the transfer.

<Warning>
Setting access to **Anyone with the link** makes your file accessible to anyone on the internet who obtains the URL — including people outside your organization. Use this setting only for content you genuinely intend to make public, and never for files containing personal data, credentials, financial information, or internal business details. When in doubt, share with specific people by email address instead.
</Warning>
